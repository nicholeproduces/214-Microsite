Zoning

Both parcels are zoned R-4B-C under the Summerhill rezoning (Z-06-24 / Ordinance 06-O-0567). The City of Atlanta has confirmed in writing that 0 South Ave SE is a legal lot of record, buildable as-of-right for a single-family structure.

Two conditions shape what can actually be built: a 20-foot minimum house width that is not variance-eligible, and corner-lot setback treatment. Consolidating the parcels satisfies the width condition. The table below summarizes each path.

If a buyer wants to What it requires
Build a duplex Rezoning to R-5. Legislative — NPU-V, Zoning Review Board, City Council. Not a variance.
Build an accessory dwelling unit with its own kitchen Rezoning to R-4, R-4A or R-5. Not permitted in R-4B-C at any size.
Build a new house on the vacant parcel standing alone A change of conditions to modify Section 6(a)'s 20-foot minimum width, AND setback variances for the roughly 11 ft x 90 ft envelope. Two separate processes.
Consolidate the two parcels City staff indicated administrative consolidation may be possible. No public hearing. Not reversible without a subdivision.
Enlarge the existing house Nothing, if the addition stays within the setbacks. A BZA variance if it does not. Section 6(a) is already satisfied — the existing house exceeds 20 feet.
Add a guest house (no kitchen, under 30% of the principal structure) Permitted as an accessory structure. Not subject to the 20-foot minimum width. Must sit behind the principal structure on a consolidated lot; placing it between the house and the street requires a BZA variance.

Buyers must independently verify all zoning, setback, and development parameters with the City of Atlanta Office of Zoning and Development.

Taxes

Georgia law taxes property at 40% of fair market value — statewide, on every property. Following a sale, the county typically resets fair market value to the purchase price for the following tax year, so a buyer’s tax bill is based on what they pay, not on the seller’s prior assessment.

About the House

I bought this house in 2010 through a neighborhood stabilization program, after looking at twenty-five others and watching several short sales fall apart. It was five years old and it looked like a dollhouse to me. Sixteen years later it’s been the most charming place I’ve lived — a historic Black neighborhood on Atlanta’s south side, a dog I adopted from across the street, and a location that has done nothing but open up. I’m selling because I’m an MBA student now, living and working between cities, and the house deserves an owner who’s here.

The first thing I thought when I saw it was that it needed a wraparound deck. Then I spent a year working nights on a TV show. Somewhere in there I built it — all 1,100 square feet.

Trees have come down since, and the sun found the deck. It gets hot up there now. It wants a pergola. That one’s for the next person.

We put in gardens during COVID. A pear tree is still out back. Things grow here.

It isn’t a finished house. It’s a house with room left in it.
